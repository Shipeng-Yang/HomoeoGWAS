## Title

*homoeogwas*: a subgenome-aware framework for genome-wide homoeolog-pair interaction GWAS in allopolyploid crops

## Abstract

Allopolyploid crops carry two or more divergent subgenomes whose homoeologous gene copies often act together in a coordinated, dosage-sensitive way, yet standard genome-wide association studies (GWAS) test markers or genes one at a time and cannot represent an interaction *between* homoeologous copies. We present *homoeogwas*, an open-source, subgenome-aware framework that tests homoeolog-pair burden-product interaction as an explicit, genome-wide estimand: it whitens the phenotype under a subgenome-stratified kinship model, aggregates each gene into a capped block burden, and tests the product of two homoeologous burdens, combining pairs genome-wide by an aggregated Cauchy test. Across allopolyploid panels spanning two ploidies, permutation inference was well calibrated (permutation median genomic-control $\lambda$ 0.97–1.00), and in simulation the test stayed type-I-calibrated under the homoeolog-burden collinearity intrinsic to the estimand — becoming conservative rather than anti-conservative at extreme collinearity under additive effects — with one identified leak, an unmodelled per-copy dominance effect that can alias into the product term at high cross-copy correlation, removed by an optional squared-burden adjustment in the identifiable regime. In hexaploid wheat, the method identified a genome-wide chromosome-1 triad interaction for heading date ($P = 2.4\times10^{-6}$; interaction effect 95% CI excluding zero) at a homoeolog pair whose independent, phenotype-free expression imbalance was robust to a detectability-matched null, with sequence-homology annotation to calmodulin-binding/IQ-domain proteins. The same *pair-only* interaction phenotype — invisible to the corresponding marginal and single-gene analyses — generalised across two further allopolyploid crops in panels not used to develop the method: two cotton fibre-quality traits ($P = 6.0\times10^{-6}$ and $1.2\times10^{-5}$) and rapeseed flowering time ($P = 3.3\times10^{-7}$), each permutation-calibrated and verified to test a truly homoeologous gene pair, and reported as statistical candidates rather than biologically validated loci. A matched-estimand benchmark and four published tools show the performance gains are estimand-specific and complementary to existing methods, and a marker-density analysis defines calibrated feasibility boundaries for when such discoveries are possible. A systematic multi-trait scan, by contrast, yielded only one new trait-specific candidate and none surviving an experiment-wide false-discovery threshold — one of several explicit negative results that bound the method's claims. By construction the method targets allopolyploids with divergent subgenomes and one-to-one homoeolog correspondence; it degrades to a standard mixed-model GWAS on diploids and does not define a homoeolog-interaction estimand for autopolyploid or mixed-ploidy panels, for which dosage-based polyploid methods are appropriate. *homoeogwas* makes homoeolog interaction reproducibly testable in allopolyploid genetics.

## Introduction

Polyploidy underlies many of the world's most important crops, including bread wheat, cotton, oilseed rape and strawberry, in which two or more divergent subgenomes were merged by allopolyploidy (Zhang et al. 2019). The duplicated *homoeologous* gene copies that result are frequently co-regulated and dosage-sensitive: homoeolog expression is often biased toward one subgenome, and the relative contribution of the two or three copies can shape phenotype (Ramírez-González et al. 2018). This raises a genetic hypothesis that conventional association mapping cannot address — that a trait may depend on the *interaction* between homoeologous copies rather than on either copy, or any single marker, alone.

Genome-wide association studies test one genetic unit at a time: a single marker (single-marker GWAS), or the aggregated rare-variant burden of a single gene (gene-based tests). Neither represents a homoeolog-pair interaction, and a genome-wide search over all SNP×SNP pairs is both underpowered, after paying its enormous multiple-testing burden, and agnostic to subgenome structure. The complementary, well-developed line of work models subgenome interactions as *variance components for prediction*: subgenome-partitioned additive and additive-by-additive (epistatic) terms, estimated by REML or by the natural-and-orthogonal-interaction parameterisation, improve genomic-selection accuracy in hexaploid wheat (Santantonio et al. 2019a, 2019b; [CITE: additional NOIA/subgenome-epistasis genomic-selection refs — user to supply]). A variance component, however, estimates an *aggregate* contribution to breeding value; it does not test, localise or error-control *which* homoeolog pair interacts. The unit-of-test question — a calibrated, genome-wide *association test* for a specific, biologically constrained homoeolog-pair interaction, as opposed to a variance term for selection — has not been addressed. Recent work has advanced *genotype construction* for polyploids — KMERIA derives dosage-aware *k*-mer genotypes directly from raw reads and tests them for association, bypassing reference alignment and variant calling [CITE: KMERIA, Nat Genet 2026] — but that advance is orthogonal to the question of what unit to test: KMERIA, like conventional single-marker GWAS, tests one genetic unit at a time, modelling within-marker allele dosage but neither partitioning variance across subgenomes nor testing an interaction between homoeologous copies. We are not aware of a tool that tests a *biologically constrained homoeolog-pair interaction* as an explicit, genome-wide estimand in allopolyploids.

We developed *homoeogwas* to fill this gap. The method defines the estimand — the product of two homoeologous gene burdens, tested within a subgenome-stratified mixed model — and provides an open, reproducible software implementation that makes it genome-wide-discoverable. Its scope is deliberate: the estimand requires divergent subgenomes with one-to-one homoeolog correspondence, so the framework is designed for allopolyploids (of any subgenome number); on a diploid it reduces to a standard mixed-model GWAS (subgenome count of one), and it is not intended for autopolyploids or mixed-ploidy panels, whose near-identical copies and continuous allele dosage are the domain of dosage-based polyploid methods. In what follows we (i) describe the framework and its statistical core; (ii) establish that its inference is calibrated across ploidies and species and controls type-I under the homoeolog collinearity the estimand creates; (iii) report a wheat triad interaction whose homoeolog pair converges with an independent homoeolog-expression signal; (iv) show that the same pair-only interaction phenotype generalises across cotton and rapeseed as calibrated candidates; (v) position the method against a matched-estimand benchmark and published tools; and (vi) provide a marker-density design analysis that predicts, before a study is run, when such discoveries are feasible. Throughout, we deliberately separate what the method *detects* from what is biologically *validated*, and report negative and boundary results explicitly.

## Results

### R1 — *homoeogwas*: a subgenome-aware framework for homoeolog-pair interaction GWAS

*homoeogwas* makes homoeolog-pair interaction a first-class, genome-wide-testable estimand in allopolyploids — a unit of test that, to our knowledge, no existing tool targets (Fig. 1; substantiated against real published software in Fig. 5 and Supplementary Fig. S8). Allopolyploid crops carry two or more divergent subgenomes whose homoeologous gene copies act in a coordinated, dosage-sensitive manner (Ramírez-González et al. 2018), yet conventional GWAS tests each marker or gene one at a time and is therefore blind to a phenotypic signal residing in the *interaction* between homoeologous copies rather than in either copy alone.

The methodological core is a subgenome-stratified linear mixed model (LMM). The phenotype is whitened under a null LMM with one genomic-relationship kinship per subgenome, $\{K_\text{sub}\}$ (all of A, B and D retained in hexaploids), and the *product* of two column-standardized gene burdens is tested in the whitened generalized-least-squares model $y_w \sim 1 + b_X + b_Y + b_X\!\cdot\!b_Y$, with per-pair $p$-values combined genome-wide by the aggregated Cauchy test (ACAT; Liu et al. 2019) and hexaploid triads tested as the ACAT omnibus of their three pairwise interactions (Fig. 1b). The LMM itself is a standard tool; our contribution is its subgenome-stratified use together with the homoeolog burden-product (Hadamard) estimand, which is what makes a *between-copy* interaction identifiable and genome-wide-discoverable rather than a variance term for prediction (Santantonio et al. 2019a, 2019b). The workflow is four reproducible, YAML-driven stages (`split → fit → interact → calibrate`; Fig. 1a), ships a tested implementation (31/31 interaction-engine unit tests, 358 passing tests overall) with a committed configuration for every analysis here, and scales from SNP-array to whole-genome panels ($4.98\times10^{5}$–$8.6\times10^{7}$ variants). We applied it to five allopolyploid species across two ploidies (Main Table 1): genome-wide permutation-calibrated discovery scans in hexaploid wheat (AABBDD), tetraploid cotton (AADD) and tetraploid rapeseed (AACC, whole-genome BnaGVD panel); two sparse-genotyping calibration boundaries — hexaploid oat (AACCDD) and a sparse genotyping-by-sequencing rapeseed panel that we contrast against its whole-genome counterpart as a within-species design test (R6); and tetraploid peanut (AABB) as an additional allotetraploid demonstration; an optional dual-GPU stack supplies zero-shot deep-learning variant priors (Supplementary Note [DL]).

### R2 — Permutation-calibrated inference, stable across ploidies and robust to homoeolog collinearity

Across the permutation-calibrated panels the genome-wide null was well calibrated, the permutation-derived median genomic-control $\lambda$ lying close to one for every discovery-feasible scan (cotton 0.99–1.00, wheat 0.985, oat 0.989, whole-genome rapeseed 0.97–0.98; Fig. 2a, Supplementary Table S1), neither inflated nor deflated; the only excluded mode is the sparse genotyping-by-sequencing rapeseed boundary panel (14 callable pairs in total), whose degenerate body/same scan mode leaves just two ($G=2$) so that $\lambda$ is uninterpretable — a marker-density boundary consistent with R6. The calibration rests on three safeguards (Methods): the subgenome-stratified kinship null that whitens both phenotype mean and burden-product design, an inverse-normal-transformed (INT) primary statistic robust to the heavy-tailed phenotypes common in these panels, and a covariate-aware Freedman–Lane permutation. In simulation under the fitted kinship null the per-pair test held nominal size (type-I $\approx 0.05$) with family-wise control after per-pair Bonferroni, and in the sparse-interaction regime it recovered the signal with substantially higher power than a single *global* homoeolog variance-component kernel while staying size-correct — per-pair resolution, not a larger omnibus, carries the signal (Fig. 2b–d, Supplementary Tables S5–S8); a parametric bootstrap gave concordant tails. Because homoeologous burdens are correlated, an unmodelled per-copy dominance effect can alias into the product term as cross-copy correlation rises; a dedicated collinearity/dominance simulation showed the unadjusted test stays calibrated except under an adversarial dominance main effect at high cross-copy correlation, where an opt-in squared-burden adjustment (`dominance_adjust`, off by default so existing results are byte-identical) restores control in the identifiable regime (Fig. 7a–d, Supplementary Tables S2–S3). In the real scans, however, cross-copy burden correlation is low (median $|r|$ 0.04–0.11; >99% of callable pairs below the $|r|=0.85$ non-identifiable threshold; Fig. 7e), so this leak regime is rarely entered: we therefore report the unadjusted INT product test as the primary discovery scan and apply `dominance_adjust` as a prespecified sensitivity check for pairs with high cross-copy correlation or design condition number, under which the lead discoveries remain past their own Bonferroni threshold in the corresponding scan context (the wheat point shown for its chromosome-1-region scan; Fig. 7f). Any signal reported below is therefore extreme under the fitted subgenome-structured null rather than an artefact of structure, outliers, collinearity or multiple testing.

### R3 — A wheat triad interaction whose homoeolog pair is expression-imbalanced

In hexaploid wheat (AABBDD; Watkins panel, $n=827$ phenotyped), the genetically-interacting chromosome-1 homoeolog pair is also the expression-imbalanced pair, providing an orthogonal, phenotype-free line of corroboration. For heading date (days to ear emergence, `Hd_dto`), a chr1 triad showed a genome-wide B–D burden-product interaction ($\beta=-0.85$, 95% CI $-1.21$ to $-0.50$, $P=2.4\times10^{-6}$) with the A–B and A–D pairs null — the signature of a two-copy interaction within a three-copy triad; both wheat interactions clear within-scan genome-wide significance (Bonferroni $0.05/G$, $G=12{,}768$ triads; genome-wide minimum-$P$ family-wise error permutation-controlled at $P_\text{FWER}=0.042$) and the interaction is robust to collinearity (genome-wide VIF 1.57) and to conditioning on the strongest local additive SNPs and PCs within $\pm1$ Mb ($\le8\%$ attenuation; Fig. 3a,c,f, Main Table 2, Supplementary Tables S16, S17, S20, S21; Supplementary Figs. S1 [genome-wide calibration], S4 [burden-cap sensitivity]). Using only an independent multi-study RNA-seq compendium (Borrill et al. 2016; Ramírez-González et al. 2018), the chr1 triad is B-dominant (subgenome-B transcript fraction $\approx 0.71$) in 8 of 11 studies that express it in the reproductive-spike window, and this imbalance is not a detectability artefact — against background triads matched on expression level, variability, missingness, gene length and callable-SNP count it sits at the 98.5–98.8th percentile (matched-null empirical $P=0.014$–0.020; Fig. 3d,e, Supplementary Tables S18, S22; Supplementary Figs. S3 [window robustness], S6 [expression-composition ternary]). By contrast the independent chr5 A–D positive is expression-balanced, and we make no convergence claim there. The interaction is also trait-specific — of 14 Watkins traits only heading date and grain width reach genome-wide significance — and the top interaction-ranked triads show no genome-wide enrichment for expression imbalance against a matched null (all $P\ge0.37$), so the chr1 locus stands alone rather than reflecting a pan-trait or genome-wide artefact (Supplementary Tables S24, S27; Supplementary Fig. S7). By sequence homology only (Ensembl Plants Pfam/PANTHER and BLASTp against *Arabidopsis* TAIR10), the chr1 copies are IQ-domain/IQM-like calmodulin-binding-protein homologs (52–61% identity; Fig. 3b, Supplementary Table S23), an interpretation we deliberately limit: because the bias is largely constitutive and the atlas is a reference genotype rather than the GWAS individuals, we present this as a triangulation of three independent legs (a genetic two-copy interaction, a matched-null-robust expression asymmetry of the same pair, and survival of local-LD conditioning), explicitly not as expression mediation, stage-specific convergence or a causal chain. The discovery was made by the research pipeline and recomputed bit-for-bit by the production CLI as an internal software-reproducibility check — not an independent same-locus replication, which remains outstanding (Methods).

### R4 — Cross-species generalization: pair-only interaction candidates in cotton and rapeseed

The pair-only interaction phenotype is not unique to wheat or to its ploidy. In two further, tetraploid allopolyploid crops — cotton and rapeseed — the scan recovered calibrated, single-locus-invisible homoeolog-pair interactions in panels distinct from those used to develop the method, though here without the orthogonal expression triangulation available for wheat.

In tetraploid cotton (AADD) we applied the scan to the CottonGVD upland-cotton diversity panel (*Gossypium hirsutum*; $n=1{,}245$ accessions with four fibre-quality BLUEs; $1.12\times10^{6}$ common SNPs, minor-allele frequency $\ge0.1$, on the Gh_CRI v1 reference, GCA_006980745.1) — an independent panel three times larger than, and genetically distinct from, the array panel used in the matched-estimand benchmark (R5). Defining homoeologs genome-wide by full-proteome reciprocal-best-hit orthology and retaining only pairs in which **both** copies carried $\ge3$ callable SNPs (Methods), 3,757 true one-to-one A–D homoeolog pairs were testable (16.3% of 22,998 curated pairs; no paralog substitution; Supplementary Table S48). Two of the four fibre traits carried genome-wide homoeolog-pair interactions invisible to the corresponding single-gene tests: fibre length at *Gh_A05G118800*|*Gh_D05G131200* (interaction $P=6.0\times10^{-6}$) and fibre elongation at *Gh_A01G025100*|*Gh_D01G022200* ($P=1.2\times10^{-5}$; Fig. 4b, Main Table 2; each lead locus with its in/near-gene SNP consequences in Supplementary Figs. [cotton fibre-length locus] and [cotton fibre-elongation locus]). Both were *pair-only* — neither single-copy gene burden approached significance (marginal $P>0.5$ for all four copies; Fig. 4h). Both scans were well calibrated under a covariate-aware Freedman–Lane permutation (permutation-derived genomic-control $\lambda=0.99$ for each trait; adding ten genotype principal components left $\lambda$ unchanged, consistent with the subgenome-stratified kinship null already absorbing structure — so the modest excess of the *observed* statistic over the theoretical null is not explained by residual stratification under the permutation null, and the permutation $\lambda$, not the theoretical-$\chi^2$ $\lambda_\text{obs}$, is the calibration arbiter for this test; Fig. 4i, Supplementary Table S46). Against the permutation null each lead was genome-wide significant **within its trait scan** (family-wise-error permutation $P=0.018$ and 0.036, paying explicitly for the 3,757 pairs tested). Across the four-trait grid both interactions survive a Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery threshold of 0.10 but not a Bonferroni/experiment-wide threshold of 0.05; we therefore present them as **proof-of-concept** discoveries — trait-wise genome-wide significant and permutation-calibrated, modest under the strictest experiment-wide accounting. Two scope conditions apply: the inverse-normal-transformed (INT) product statistic is the primary endpoint (the untransformed statistic is mildly anti-conservative — raw $\lambda_\text{obs}$ 1.10 and 1.23 for fibre length and elongation — and is reported only as a sensitivity check, not as calibration-controlled per-pair inference), and because the public CottonGVD genotypes are filtered to common variants (MAF $\ge0.1$) these are *common-variant* interactions, any rarer-variant contribution to the same estimand requiring unfiltered calls. A pre-registered same-locus replication in an independent whole-genome-sequenced upland-cotton panel (HBAU/NDM8, $n=419$; genetically distinct — maximum cross-panel identity-by-state 0.36 over allele-harmonised bridge-lifted markers, no duplicate accessions) did not corroborate either lead: the fibre-length A copy carried no callable SNP in that assembly (non-testable, reported rather than paralog-substituted), and the fibre-elongation pair returned a null (omniB permutation $P=0.35$ versus discovery $1.2\times10^{-5}$). The test was pre-specified as underpowered — the discovery effect (interaction PVE 1.5%) sits below the panel's 80%-power detection limit (2.2%) — so this is *inconclusive* rather than a refutation; an engine-path positive control (a strong injected interaction recovered at $P<10^{-60}$) and the same scan's ten other elongation pairs at $P<10^{-3}$ confirm the null is a property of the pair, not the pipeline. A post-hoc diagnostic located the cause in the panels themselves: none of the discovery burden's variants is present in the replication panel, and restricting the replication burden to common variants (matching the discovery filter) leaves it null, so the non-replication is consistent with either a winner's-curse false positive or a real effect carried by CottonGVD-private variants — an intrinsic limit of same-locus replication for common-variant burden interactions across genetically distinct panels (Supplementary Table [cotton replication]; pre-registration frozen before the mapping, git blob 3b675e8).

The scan recovered an independent calibrated candidate in a third species, allotetraploid rapeseed (*Brassica napus*, AACC). On the dense BnaGVD whole-genome panel (*n*=926 accessions with a flowering-time record; $1.8\times10^{6}$ SNPs on the Darmor-bzh reference), the same reciprocal-best-hit orthology and $\ge3$-callable-SNP gate yielded 17,404 testable A–C homoeolog pairs (80.8% coverage; no paralog substitution; Supplementary Table S48) — a more than thousand-fold increase over the 14 callable pairs left by the sparse genotyping-by-sequencing panel of the *same* species (Horvath et al. 2020), a within-species density contrast we return to as a prospective test of the design model in R6. For flowering time, a single A–C pair, *BnaA02g19610D*|*BnaC02g22960D*, reached genome-wide significance (interaction $P=3.3\times10^{-7}$, past the Bonferroni threshold $0.05/17{,}404=2.9\times10^{-6}$; permutation family-wise-error $P=0.008$; ten genotype PCs included as covariates; Fig. 4a,c, Main Table 2), with the two immediately adjacent homoeolog pairs *BnaA02g19700D*|*BnaC02g23040D* and *BnaA02g19730D*|*BnaC02g23100D* also among the top-ranked ($P=4.4\times10^{-6}$ and $2.8\times10^{-5}$), a regional statistical concordance that argues against a single-pair artefact (Fig. 4d; a robustness observation, not biological corroboration). The interaction was again *pair-only* — neither single-copy burden approached genome-wide significance (subgenome-A $P\approx0.86$, subgenome-C $P\approx0.04$, both far above the $2.9\times10^{-6}$ threshold; the genome-wide single-copy marginal $p$-distribution is uniform, 5.0% of pairs below 0.05 exactly as expected under the null, so the nominally lower C-copy value carries no marginal signal), yet the product reached $3\times10^{-7}$ — a textbook single-locus-invisible signal (Fig. 4e). It was well calibrated (INT permutation $\lambda=0.98$; the untransformed statistic was mildly inflated at $\lambda_\text{obs}=1.11$, so the INT analysis is primary; Fig. 4f) and robust to population structure: conditioning the scan, on top of ten genotype principal components, additionally on the winter/spring/semi-winter ecotype and on local BnaA02 *FLC*-region additive tag SNPs left the lead genome-wide significant throughout ($P=3.3\times10^{-7}\!\to\!4.1\times10^{-7}\!\to\!8.1\times10^{-7}$, remaining past its Bonferroni threshold and rank 1; Fig. 4g, Supplementary Table S47), so the flowering-time interaction is not an artefact of the ecotype/*FLC* structure that dominates rapeseed flowering-time variation. The remaining four phenotypes (two seed-oil-content and two trichome measures, $n=269$) were honest nulls (no pair past Bonferroni; permutation family-wise-error $P$ 0.17–0.35; $\lambda\approx1$), the oil-content scans being power-limited rather than evidence of no interaction, with all four null-trait scans calibrated ($\lambda_\text{perm}\approx0.97$–0.98) and no pair reaching genome-wide significance (Supplementary Table S43; Supplementary Fig. [rapeseed null-trait QQ]).

None of these cotton or rapeseed leads is supported by direct functional validation, and only one carries a partial expression correlate; we report each honestly. In all three leads, reciprocal-best-hit orthology confirms the tested pair is a true one-to-one homoeolog rather than a mis-paired paralog, both copies mapping to a single *Arabidopsis* ortholog: the rapeseed pair to AT1G80580 (an AP2/ERF-family transcription factor), the cotton fibre-length pair to a MYB-like/homeodomain transcription factor (AT3G16350/AT5G47390, 56–57% identity; the cotton copies are annotated MYBS3), and the cotton fibre-elongation pair to a glyoxalase I (lactoylglutathione lyase, AT2G32090, 76% identity) — direct evidence that the pairing step recovers genuine homoeologs (Supplementary Table S49). The lead pairs differ in the character of their in-gene variation (Supplementary Table S45): the rapeseed pair carries coding (missense) SNPs, the cotton fibre-length pair is intron/regulatory-biased with no missense variant, and the fibre-elongation pair is intermediate — an incidental annotation contrast, not a mechanistic claim. The wheat chr1 pair (R3) is verified on a different and stronger basis — curated 1:1:1 HCTriad synteny (four-method consensus: OrthoFinder, BLAST reciprocal-best-hit, i-ADHoRe and MCScanX) rather than a single reciprocal-best-hit run — and, consistent with lineage expansion in *Arabidopsis*, its two interacting copies map to distinct but paralogous *Arabidopsis* IQ-domain calmodulin-binding genes (AT2G26190 and AT4G33050) rather than to one ortholog, so the shared-single-ortholog criterion is neither expected nor required there (Supplementary Table S49). The homology annotation and the fibre expression diverge in an instructive way. MYB-family transcription factors are established regulators of cotton fibre development (GhMYB25, GhMYB25-like; Machado et al. 2009; Walford et al. 2011; Zhao et al. 2024), so the fibre-length pair lies in a trait-relevant family by homology — although its closest characterised homolog, MYBS3, is itself a cold-response MYB in rice (Su et al. 2010) rather than a fibre-development MYB, so the family relevance is broad rather than specific. Consistent with that caveat, in two independent upland-cotton fibre developmental RNA-seq series (Zhang et al. 2015; Hu et al. 2019) both of its copies are only weakly expressed in elongating fibre and peak earlier, in pre-anthesis ovule (the initiation window), consistent with MYB regulators acting at fibre initiation but not corroborating the elongation-stage signal. Conversely the fibre-elongation glyoxalase pair is genuinely expressed in fibre with both homoeologs co-expressed and rising to a peak at ~20 days post-anthesis (the late-elongation/secondary-wall window) in both datasets, and at that peak the two copies are approximately subgenome-balanced (subgenome-A expression fraction 0.51–0.52 in both series, though more D-biased at earlier fibre stages; Fig. 4j, Supplementary Tables S44 and S44b) — consistent with both homoeologs contributing at the trait-relevant window rather than one copy dominating there. Although we found no report of a direct cotton-fibre function for this specific gene, glyoxalase I is a glutathione-dependent methylglyoxal-detoxification enzyme (Batth et al. 2020) from a family characterised genome-wide in *Gossypium hirsutum* (Xu et al. 2023), and redox homeostasis has been shown to promote cotton fibre elongation (Tian et al. 2024), so the pair sits, by homology and expression stage, near a trait-relevant redox axis — a modest developmental correlate, albeit broadly expressed rather than fibre-specific, and from reference genotypes rather than the GWAS individuals. The rapeseed AP2/ERF pair sits at a novel position (A02:12.2 Mb, far from the A02 *BnaFLC* locus) and is near-silent in independent *B. napus* seed, shoot-apex and leaf RNA-seq (TPM 0.02–0.17), so it has no expression-level support. We therefore present all three leads as calibrated, single-locus-invisible **candidates** resting primarily on statistical, calibration and pairing-verification evidence — with at most a partial expression correlate (cotton fibre elongation) and no functional validation — not as biologically validated discoveries; functional or paired-data validation, as for the unresolved wheat same-locus replication (Discussion), is the priority next step rather than a completed claim. In the homoeolog-interaction network view the cotton and rapeseed leads stand out among otherwise sparse A–D / A–C edges (Fig. 8).

### R5 — An estimand-specific advantage over published tools

A matched-estimand benchmark shows that the advantage of *homoeogwas* is estimand-specific rather than universal, and a marker-density analysis turns the method into a prospective design tool. In a matched candidate-pair benchmark where six method families test the *same* synteny-guided pair universe, each at its own held-out family-wise error rate (0.046–0.06), *homoeogwas* was the most powerful for the gene-resolution, sign-coherent, multi-SNP burden-product estimand (power 0.36/0.82/1.00 at interaction PVE 0.05/0.10/0.20), a signal to which single-gene burden and marginal GWAS were essentially blind; but a SNP×SNP epistasis test won when the truth was a single causal SNP pair, all methods were near-powerless for sign-discordant interactions, an additive-only control kept *homoeogwas* power at $\approx 0$ (no additive-to-epistasis leakage), and deliberately mispaired homoeologs gave zero power (pair specificity; Fig. 5a,b, Supplementary Tables S28–S30). The same estimand mismatch appears in a post-discovery, lead-locus benchmark of published software on the CottonGVD leads. This analysis is conditioned on our discoveries — the tools are handed the exact lead genes and their in-gene SNPs — so it asks whether published single-marker, single-gene and unguided SNP×SNP estimands recover the same frozen signal under their natural multiplicity, not whether they would win an unbiased genome-wide contest (that controlled comparison is the matched simulation above). Structure-corrected single-marker tests (GWASpoly with its polyploid kinship, Rosyara et al. 2016; regenie single-variant with a step-1 polygenic null, Mbatchou et al. 2021) and structure-corrected single-gene aggregation tests (regenie SKAT-O/ACAT-O burden; SKAT, Wu et al. 2011, and STAAR, Li et al. 2020, each adjusted for ten genome-wide genotype principal components) detected neither interaction under prespecified thresholds: the best in-region single-gene signal ($P\approx0.01$–0.05, at the D copy, with the A copy null) is two to three orders of magnitude weaker than the interaction and never recovers the between-copy product (Fig. 5c, Supplementary Table S31). Two further observations sharpen the point. First, without structure correction the same single-gene rare-variant tests returned spuriously extreme $p$-values ($10^{-38}$ to $10^{-43}$) that collapsed to non-significance under either genome-wide or subgenome-only principal components (structure-inflated uncorrected runs, Supplementary Fig. S8) — behaviour consistent with population-structure confounding on this 1,245-accession panel, which the subgenome-stratified kinship model is designed to control (its calibration is shown in R2). Second, a PLINK (Chang et al. 2015) SNP×SNP epistasis scan illustrated the resolution–multiplicity trade-off: its raw top fibre-elongation pair was itself structure-inflated (uncorrected $P=3\times10^{-17}$), and re-testing all candidate A×D SNP pairs with the same principal components attenuated the best to $P=7\times10^{-5}$ — enough to pass a candidate-region threshold but far short of the $\sim2.4\times10^{11}$-pair genome-wide SNP×SNP multiplicity ($\sim2\times10^{-13}$) — while the distributed fibre-length interaction was not recovered even in-region (best candidate-pair $P=2\times10^{-3}$). Synteny-guided gene pairing reduces that multiplicity by roughly eight orders of magnitude ($\sim2.4\times10^{11}$ SNP pairs to $\sim3{,}800$ gene pairs), which is what lets the same signal clear a genome-wide threshold (Fig. 5d). These tools test a *different estimand*, or pay a far larger multiplicity, not an inferior one, and we treat the reference-free genotyping method KMERIA [CITE: KMERIA, Nat Genet 2026] as a complementary genotype-construction layer rather than a head-to-head comparator.

### R6 — Marker-density design guidance and boundary conditions

Finally, because pair testing needs enough in-gene markers in *both* copies, a design model predicts the callable-pair count $G$ from marker density (held-out median relative error 3.5%) and is consistent with the real panels: dense whole-genome or common-SNP panels (wheat WGS, CottonGVD cotton, BnaGVD rapeseed) are discovery-feasible and yielded candidate interactions, whereas sparse genotyping-by-sequencing (oat, and the sparse Horvath rapeseed panel) falls below the boundary (Fig. 6a, Supplementary Tables S32–S36, Supplementary Fig. S36). Because the callable-pair count depends on the pairing mode, we report each sparse panel by mode under a prespecified boundary-status rule ($G<5$, excluded as degenerate with calibration and power uninterpretable; $5\le G<70$, testable-boundary — calibration and null reported but no discovery claim; $70\le G<300$, borderline; $\ge300$, discovery-feasible; Supplementary Table [boundary-audit]): in oat the strict $1{:}1{:}1$ triad scan has $G=17$ callable triads whereas the pairwise flank scan across 36 traits has $G=69$ callable pairs (body mode $G=27$), all null and permutation-calibrated ($\lambda_\text{perm}\approx0.99$); in the Horvath rapeseed panel the non-degenerate flank mode has $G=14$ callable pairs and all eleven traits are null, while the body mode leaves only $G=2$ pairs and is excluded as degenerate. Rapeseed provides the cleanest, *prospective* test of the design model, because the same species was genotyped at two densities that straddle the predicted boundary: the sparse genotyping-by-sequencing panel leaves only 14 callable homoeolog pairs — below the discovery boundary, and indeed null — whereas the whole-genome BnaGVD panel crosses the boundary to 17,404 callable pairs and yielded the flowering-time interaction reported in R4. The design model, calibrated on the other panels, predicts within a single species that moving from GBS to WGS converts a non-discoverable design into a discoverable one — and the rapeseed outcome matches this predicted boundary crossing, illustrating that the design tool makes a prospective, falsifiable prediction rather than merely rationalising post hoc which panels happened to yield hits. As markers are thinned the per-pair conditional power stays high and flat ($\approx 0.98$, ~2× a global kernel) while the coverage-adjusted discovery probability collapses (0.99→0.47→0.11; Fig. 6b,c), so the bottleneck is callable-pair coverage and the remaining sparse-GBS oat null is a calibrated data-resolution boundary, not evidence that homoeolog interactions are biologically absent. Consistent with a coverage limitation rather than an uninformative scan, the *marginal* subgenome-stratified single-marker analysis of the same oat panel recovers a substantial fraction of that panel's own previously published same-variable signals — 13 of 22 published minimum-temperature-of-coldest-month (BIO6) loci and 39 of 144 soil-organic-carbon (SOC) loci at $P<5\times10^{-8}$, with mildly conservative calibration ($\lambda_\text{GC}\approx0.92$ and $0.81$) — a same-panel, single-marker method-consistency check that isolates the interaction null to the small callable-pair universe. This oat analysis is a landrace origin-environment association, not a claim of causal adaptation, and the same-panel single-marker concordance is explicitly neither an independent replication nor a homoeolog-pair result.

## Discussion

*homoeogwas* makes homoeolog-pair interaction a first-class, genome-wide-testable estimand in allopolyploids, and packages it as calibrated, reproducible, open software. The central contribution is methodological and computational rather than a single discovery: a subgenome-stratified mixed model that whitens both phenotype and design, a burden-product interaction test at gene-pair resolution, genome-wide combination by ACAT, and permutation/bootstrap calibration that we show behaves correctly under the real, structured covariance of multiple allopolyploid panels at two ploidies. A matched-estimand benchmark and four published tools demonstrate that the advantage is *estimand-specific* — existing single-marker, single-gene and unguided-epistasis methods target different units and do not detect these signals under prespecified thresholds — and a marker-density design analysis turns the method into a prospective planning tool. *homoeogwas* is also complementary to recent reference-free genotyping methods for polyploids such as KMERIA [CITE: KMERIA, Nat Genet 2026]: where KMERIA improves how genotype features are *constructed* from raw reads, *homoeogwas* changes the *estimand* used to model allopolyploid architecture. The two also occupy different ploidy scopes — KMERIA is validated mainly on diploid, autopolyploid and aneuploid panels, whereas our homoeolog-pair estimand is defined for allopolyploids with divergent subgenomes — so the methods are complementary rather than competing, and a natural future direction is to feed reference-free *k*-mer dosage genotypes into a subgenome-stratified interaction model once they are mapped to homoeolog units.

The biological discoveries are proof-of-concept rather than a broad catalogue, and we frame them as such. In cotton and rapeseed the leads are statistically separable from, and invisible to, marginal and single-gene effects, and in each the tested pair is a verified one-to-one homoeolog — both copies mapping to a single *Arabidopsis* ortholog — rather than a mis-paired paralog. This distinction is not incidental: a development-stage cotton analysis on a sparser array panel produced an apparent fibre interaction that we traced to exactly such a paralog substitution (the true homoeolog was uncallable and the pipeline had back-filled the next-best paralog) and discarded after separating the orthology and callability gates, which is why the cotton results reported here come from the larger CottonGVD panel under the corrected pairing logic. The cotton fibre-length and fibre-elongation interactions are proof-of-concept — trait-wise genome-wide significant and permutation-calibrated, modest under an experiment-wide threshold — and, because the public genotypes are filtered to common variants, are common-variant interactions; a pre-registered same-locus replication in an independent whole-genome-sequenced cotton panel was inconclusive (underpowered by design) and did not corroborate them, and a diagnostic tracing the non-replication to non-shared variants across the two panels is consistent with the leads being either winner's-curse false positives or CottonGVD-private-variant effects (Results). Annotation and expression give a deliberately mixed, instructive picture that we report without cherry-picking. By sequence homology the fibre-length pair encodes a MYB-like transcription factor — a family with established roles in cotton fibre development (GhMYB25/GhMYB25-like; Machado et al. 2009; Walford et al. 2011; Zhao et al. 2024), though its closest characterised homolog MYBS3 is a rice cold-response MYB (Su et al. 2010), so the family relevance is broad rather than specific — and in two independent fibre developmental RNA-seq series both copies are only weakly expressed in elongating fibre and peak at fibre initiation, so the homology is trait-relevant but the expression does not corroborate an elongation-stage role. Conversely the fibre-elongation glyoxalase pair is genuinely co-expressed in fibre, peaking at ~20 days post-anthesis in the trait-relevant late-elongation window in both datasets and approximately subgenome-balanced at that peak (subgenome-A fraction 0.51–0.52, though more D-biased earlier in fibre development), consistent with both homoeologs contributing at the trait-relevant window rather than one dominating; while no direct cotton-fibre function is reported for this specific gene, glyoxalase I is a glutathione-dependent methylglyoxal-detoxification enzyme (Batth et al. 2020) from a family characterised genome-wide in *Gossypium hirsutum* (Xu et al. 2023), and redox homeostasis promotes cotton fibre elongation (Tian et al. 2024), placing the pair near a trait-relevant redox axis — a modest developmental correlate, though broadly expressed and from reference genotypes rather than the GWAS individuals. The rapeseed flowering-time interaction — the statistically strongest lead across the two species (within-scan genome-wide significant by both Bonferroni and a permutation family-wise-error test) — has no *direct* expression support but is a mechanistically plausible candidate we develop below: the pair is an AP2/ERF-family transcription-factor homoeolog at a novel position (several megabases from both the A02 *BnaFLC* and *BnaFT* copies) whose two copies are near-background in independent seed, shoot-apex, leaf and abiotic-stress RNA-seq, so we do not invoke expression convergence. We therefore present these as calibrated, single-locus-invisible candidates resting on statistical, calibration and pairing-verification evidence — cotton at the proof-of-concept tier (trait-wise significant, modest experiment-wide, with a partial developmental correlate) and rapeseed as the statistically stronger, genome-wide-significant candidate — with subgenome-resolved expression and functional cross-references the priority next step rather than completed claims. In wheat, a chromosome-1 heading-date triad interaction occurs at the homoeolog pair that is also expression-imbalanced, an imbalance that survives a detectability-matched null and a local-LD conditioning test; the pair's copies are calmodulin-binding-protein (IQ-domain/IQM-like) homologs, a biologically plausible but homology-only annotation. We deliberately limit this to a *triangulation* of independent legs — genetics, a matched-null-robust expression asymmetry, and local-LD survival — and explicitly not a claim of expression mediation, stage-specific enrichment (the bias is largely constitutive), or a validated causal chain, since the expression atlas is from a reference genotype rather than the GWAS individuals. A systematic multi-trait scan in wheat further yielded only one new trait-specific candidate (grain width) and none surviving an experiment-wide false-discovery threshold, and a genome-scale test found that interaction strength does *not* track expression imbalance genome-wide — results we report as honest negatives that bound the method's claims.

A candidate mechanism for the rapeseed interaction illustrates both the promise and the limits of these leads. The rapeseed lead is a cleanly resolved one-to-one homoeolog pair — each copy is the other's reciprocal-best protein hit at 96% identity, separated from the next-best paralog by more than five-fold in bitscore, on the syntenic A02/C02 homoeologous chromosomes and the sole *B. napus* representatives of their *Arabidopsis* ortholog — so, unlike the retired development-panel cotton hit, it is not a paralog-substitution artefact. Both copies encode ERF084, an ethylene-responsive AP2/ERF transcription factor, which suggests a concrete, if unvalidated, reading of the *pair-only* signal: if the two near-identical transcription-factor copies are functionally redundant, variation in either copy could be buffered by the other, leaving both single-copy burdens non-significant and making the association detectable only through their *joint* burden state — the non-additive, single-locus-invisible signature the burden-product test is designed to detect, and distinct from an additive dosage effect (which would produce marginal associations). A route to flowering is plausible from adjacent ethylene/ERF literature, though not demonstrated for this pair: the AP2/ERF superfamily broadly mediates both stress responses and developmental programmes (Licausi et al. 2013), and specific ERF-subfamily factors touch the flowering pathway — Arabidopsis ERF1 directly inhibits *FLOWERING LOCUS T* expression to delay flowering (Chen et al. 2021), ERF12 regulates floral development (Chandler and Werr 2019), and ethylene signalling through EIN3/EIL1 can modulate *FLOWERING LOCUS C* expression (Xu et al. 2023) — the vernalisation hub governing rapeseed flowering. Consistent with a *trans*-acting factor rather than a cis-linked artefact, the pair lies several megabases from both the A02 *BnaFLC* (~4.3 Mb) and *BnaFT* (~5.8 Mb) copies and has no single-copy marginal signal, so it is not tagging those loci through linkage. We deliberately hold this as a hypothesis, not a result: the pair is near-background across the queried tissue, hormone and abiotic-stress expression atlases — a genuine tension with a protein-dosage model — and no molecular validation was performed. The rapeseed interaction is therefore a statistically robust, mechanistically plausible candidate for which a possible molecular basis — homoeolog redundancy acting through an ethylene/ERF–flowering axis — remains to be tested.

For a polyploid breeder the estimand also changes what an association scan hands back. Conventional single-marker and single-gene GWAS report loci with a marginal effect — a gene worth pursuing on its own — whereas *homoeogwas* reports homoeolog *pairs* whose interaction matters even when neither copy is marginally associated (both cotton leads had single-copy marginal $P>0.5$ yet an interaction $P<10^{-5}$); it is therefore not a more powerful version of the same test but a complementary one that recovers signal a single-gene scan is, by construction, blind to. The practical reading differs accordingly: a significant pair implies the trait depends on the *combination* of the two homoeologous copies rather than on either allele alone, so the actionable unit is the balance or joint dosage of the A- and D-copies — a decision native to allopolyploid improvement (subgenome balancing, copy stacking, or paired editing of both homoeologs) that a single-gene hit does not express — and it nominates two homology-linked candidate genes together with a specific mechanistic hypothesis (co-function, redundancy or sub-functionalisation between copies) for functional follow-up. We are equally explicit about the limit of this value: where a trait is governed by a single large-effect gene, single-marker GWAS remains simpler, better powered and more directly interpretable (Fig. 5), and the interactions reported here are calibrated, proof-of-concept candidates that, like any association signal, require allele-specific-expression, near-isogenic-line or perturbation validation before they can guide selection. The contribution is thus to widen the space of breeder-actionable signal — not to displace single-gene mapping, but to make a polyploid-native class of interaction discoverable and interpretable.

Several boundaries are explicit. The per-pair test's power advantage over a global kernel is concentrated in the low-effect, per-pair-separable regime; we do not claim universal superiority. The method's domain of validity follows from its estimand. Because a homoeolog-pair interaction is only defined where two divergent subgenomes carry one-to-one homoeologous copies, *homoeogwas* applies to allopolyploids (the interaction engine generalises to any subgenome number $n\ge2$, tested empirically for tetraploids and hexaploids and an extrapolation for octoploids); on a diploid panel it degrades gracefully to a standard subgenome-agnostic mixed-model GWAS, which we used as a generalisation check. It is, by construction, *not* a method for autopolyploids or mixed-ploidy panels: autopolyploid copies are near-identical rather than divergent, cannot be assigned to distinct subgenomes or paired one-to-one, and carry continuous allele dosage, so the burden-product estimand has no counterpart there — that regime is the domain of dosage-aware polyploid genotyping and association methods, to which our framework is complementary. Segmental allopolyploids fall in between and are testable only on the subset of cleanly one-to-one homoeolog pairs. The sparse-genotyping panels (oat, and the sparse genotyping-by-sequencing rapeseed panel) are calibrated data-resolution boundaries — too few callable homoeolog pairs for genome-wide discovery — not evidence that interactions are biologically absent; rapeseed makes this concrete, since the same species crosses from below the boundary on its sparse panel to a genome-wide discovery on its whole-genome panel, exactly as the design model predicts. Two extensions remain supportive rather than central: a zero-shot deep-learning variant prior did not measurably improve ranking on any panel within the candidate universe, and the global homoeolog kernel is retained only as a comparator. No discovery is supported by orthogonal functional or wet-lab validation; the orthogonal evidence we do provide is observational — for the wheat chr1 B–D interaction, a phenotype-free, detectability-matched-null homoeolog-expression asymmetry of the same pair together with local-LD conditioning (the triangulation above). Strict same-locus replication is not currently feasible with available public data. We attempted to re-test the wheat chr1 B–D interaction in the independent WHEALBI collection (same IWGSC v1.0 coordinates), but its exome-capture genotyping leaves the D-subgenome copy of the pair with a single callable in-gene SNP — below the three-SNP burden threshold — so the interaction is not estimable there, and we were unable to identify another public panel that combines whole-genome (rather than exome- or array-) genotyping with a matched heading-date phenotype. We therefore designate independent same-locus replication, and orthogonal functional validation (allele-specific expression, near-isogenic lines or perturbation) of a lead interaction, as the priority next steps rather than completed claims.

A fourth allopolyploid, tetraploid peanut (*Arachis hypogaea*, AABB; PeanutPan panel, $n=230$ phenotyped), provides an end-to-end generality demonstration on a subgenome topology distinct from wheat, cotton and rapeseed. The subgenome-stratified variance partition behaved sensibly and was well calibrated ($\lambda_\text{GC}\approx1$): 100-seed weight partitioned across both subgenomes (subgenome-A/B variance fractions $\approx0.48$/$0.33$) whereas seed length was A-subgenome-dominated ($\approx0.66$/$0.05$). As a same-panel method-consistency check, the seed-length marginal lead (chromosome A05, $\approx6.15$ Mb) fell $\sim27$ kb from a previously published seed-length association *in the same panel* — a concordance anchor on the same data, explicitly not an independent replication and not a homoeolog-pair result. The homoeolog-pair interaction scan, by contrast, was a calibrated negative: across 379 callable A–B homoeolog pairs neither seed trait yielded a significant interaction (genome-wide ACAT $P=0.65$ and $0.78$; permutation $\lambda\approx0.97$, slightly conservative), showing that the test does not manufacture interactions in a new allopolyploid. Because the public panel exposes only three per-accession phenotypes, this is a bounded demonstration and calibrated negative rather than a claim that homoeolog interactions are biologically absent in peanut.

These limitations define the natural next steps: expanding the homoeolog-pair universe beyond curated one-to-one triads as a pre-registered secondary search; an independent replication cohort with whole-genome genotyping and matched phenotypes; an empirical octoploid (strawberry) test to close the ploidy-generality loop; and orthogonal validation — allele-specific expression, near-isogenic lines or perturbation — of a lead interaction. Even without these, *homoeogwas* provides the allopolyploid-genetics community with a calibrated, reproducible, estimand-specific tool and a design framework for when homoeolog-interaction discovery is feasible.

## Methods

**Panels and genotypes.** We analysed five allopolyploid species (six genotype panels, counting both rapeseed genotyping densities) — discovery scans in wheat, cotton and rapeseed, two sparse-genotyping calibration boundaries (oat and a second, sparse rapeseed panel), and tetraploid peanut as an additional allotetraploid demonstration (subgenome variance decomposition and a calibrated-negative interaction scan; Discussion): hexaploid wheat (*Triticum aestivum*, AABBDD; Watkins landrace collection, $n\approx827$ phenotyped of 1,051 genotyped, whole-genome sequencing, IWGSC RefSeq v1.0 coordinates `chr1A–chr7D`) [CITE: Watkins]; tetraploid cotton (*Gossypium hirsutum*, AADD) in two panels — the CottonGVD upland-cotton diversity panel ($n=1{,}245$, $1.12\times10^{6}$ common SNPs at minor-allele frequency $\ge0.1$, Gh_CRI v1 reference GCA_006980745.1) [CITE: CottonGVD, Fang et al. 2021] used for genome-wide discovery, and the HBAU NDM8 array panel ($n=419$, $4.98\times10^{5}$-SNP array, assembly GCA_018997965.1) [CITE: cotton panel] used for the matched-estimand benchmark and power simulations (R5); tetraploid rapeseed (*Brassica napus*, AACC) likewise in two panels — the whole-genome BnaGVD diversity panel ($n=926$ with a flowering-time record, $1.8\times10^{6}$ SNPs, Darmor-bzh / Darmor v4.1 reference) [CITE: BnaGVD, Yang et al. 2021] used for genome-wide discovery, and the sparse genotyping-by-sequencing Horvath panel ($n=379$, $\sim2.9\times10^{5}$ imputed GBS SNPs, Darmor v4.1) (Horvath et al. 2020) retained as a marker-density boundary and within-species design contrast (R6); hexaploid oat (*Avena sativa*, AACCDD; $n=737$, genotyping-by-sequencing) [CITE: oat]; and tetraploid peanut (*Arachis hypogaea*, AABB; PeanutPan 269 panel, $n=230$, whole-genome sequencing, NDH108 reference) [CITE: Lu 2025]. An octoploid strawberry eQTL panel (*Fragaria* × *ananassa*, 8n; no phenotype record) is *not* one of these analysis panels; it enters only as an 8n feasibility-boundary anchor in the marker-density design analysis (Fig. 6), where its callable-pair count is too low for genome-wide discovery. Variants were assigned to subgenomes from the chromosome of origin. **Phenotypes** were BLUEs/cross-environment means; the wheat trait is heading date (days to ear emergence, `Hd_dto`, Zadoks GS55); cotton discovery traits are four fibre-quality BLUEs (fibre length, strength, micronaire and elongation; the HBAU benchmark panel carries a broader fibre-quality set); the rapeseed discovery trait is flowering time (days), with seed-oil-content and trichome scores as additional, honestly-null rapeseed phenotypes.

**Subgenome-stratified LMM and whitening.** For each subgenome $s$ we built an additive genomic relationship matrix $K_s$ from mean-imputed, standardised dosages ($ZZ^\top/m$, trace-normalised). We fit a multi-kinship REML null $y \sim 1 + \sum_s u_s + \varepsilon$, $u_s\sim N(0,\sigma_s^2 K_s)$, retaining *every* subgenome (all of A, B, D in hexaploids), and formed the whitener $V^{-1/2}$ from $V=\sum_s\hat\sigma_s^2 K_s + \hat\sigma_e^2 I$ by eigendecomposition. The phenotype primary statistic is the rank-based inverse-normal transform (INT), $\mathrm{INT}(y_i)=\Phi^{-1}\big((\mathrm{rank}(y_i)-0.5)/n\big)$, applied before whitening; the untransformed (raw) trait is a sensitivity analysis run through the identical GRM/whitening pipeline. The INT primary versus raw sensitivity contrast is what exposes, and removes, outlier-driven inflation on heavy-tailed phenotypes: on the BnaGVD rapeseed flowering-time scan the untransformed statistic is mildly inflated (genomic-control $\lambda_\text{obs}=1.11$, with a spuriously extreme raw top pair) whereas the INT statistic is calibrated ($\lambda=0.98$) and carries the genome-wide flowering-time hit, so the INT analysis is primary; on the rapeseed null traits (and on the sparse Horvath panel) the INT statistic likewise sits at $\lambda\approx1$, there with no surviving hit (Supplementary Table S40).

**Gene burdens and the homoeolog-pair interaction test.** Each gene's in-region SNPs (gene body, or body ±2 kb flank) were aggregated into a block burden = mean of column-standardised dosages, capping the number of SNPs at 150 (deterministic subsampling) to prevent whole-genome-sequencing SNP density from diluting the mean; genes with $<3$ callable SNPs were dropped. For a homoeolog pair $(X,Y)$ we tested the interaction in a whitened generalised-least-squares model $y_w \sim 1 + b_X + b_Y + b_X\!\cdot\!b_Y$, taking the two-sided $t$-test on the product coefficient. Hexaploid triads were tested as the three pairwise interactions (A–B, A–D, B–D) and an omnibus combination. **Genome-wide combination** used the aggregated Cauchy association test (ACAT) (Liu et al. 2019), which is robust to dependence; the per-triad omnibus is the ACAT of the three pairwise $p$-values. Homoeolog pairs were defined from curated one-to-one(-to-one) orthology where available (wheat HCTriads; Ramírez-González et al. 2018), and otherwise from full-proteome reciprocal-best-hit orthology (DIAMOND) — for cotton against the Gh_CRI v1 proteome and for rapeseed against the Darmor-bzh proteome — each filtered to one-to-one A–D / A–C correspondences. Crucially, orthology and callability were treated as *separate* gates: a pair entered the test only if it was a true reciprocal-best-hit homoeolog *and* both copies carried $\ge3$ callable SNPs; pairs failing the callability gate were dropped rather than back-filled with the next-best (paralogous) partner, so no paralog was ever substituted for an uncallable true homoeolog (cotton 16.3% / rapeseed 80.8% of curated pairs retained; reported coverage in each panel). The framework is ploidy-general: a group of $s$ subgenomes is tested as its $\binom{s}{2}$ pairwise interactions plus a group-level ACAT omnibus (tested empirically for tetraploids and hexaploids; higher ploidies are a structural extension, and the test remains pairwise rather than a joint $n$-way interaction). The homoeolog-interaction network (Fig. 8) is a descriptive visualisation of these per-pair tests — each ortholog group is a polygon whose vertices are subgenomes and whose edges are weighted by the per-pair interaction $-\log_{10}p$ — not an independently tested graph statistic. **Dosage decomposition.** To attribute a hit to additive dosage versus epistasis, each pair signal was reparameterised into total-dosage (S), imbalance (I) and epistasis (Q = $b_X\!\cdot\!b_Y$) components and the three tested separately under the same whitened model (Supplementary Tables S12, S19).

**Calibration and multiple testing.** We distinguish, and report separately, three levels of multiple-testing control. (i) *Within-scan genome-wide*: within each (trait × mode) scan the bar was Bonferroni $0.05/G$ over primary units, plus an empirical permutation $p$ and an acceptable permutation $\lambda$. (ii) *Post-selection genome-wide*: a bootstrap $p$ from the fitted kinship null that explicitly pays for selecting the best unit over all callable units within a scan, reported under both the minimum-$p$ and the genome-wide-ACAT statistics. (iii) *Experiment-wide*: across the full trait grid (all traits within a panel), by Benjamini–Hochberg FDR and, more conservatively, by Bonferroni. The headline panels are not symmetric across these levels and we report each on the criterion it actually meets: the wheat heading-date interaction and the rapeseed flowering-time interaction each clear level (i) within-scan genome-wide significance (Bonferroni $0.05/G$ plus a permutation-controlled genome-wide family-wise error — wheat $P_\text{FWER}=0.042$, rapeseed $P_\text{FWER}=0.008$), whereas the cotton fibre-quality interactions clear within-scan significance (permutation family-wise error $P=0.018$ and 0.036 for fibre length and fibre elongation) and a BH-FDR of 0.10 across the four-trait grid but not an experiment-wide Bonferroni at 0.05, so we present the cotton hits as proof-of-concept. For no panel do we assert a single experiment-wide threshold spanning every species × trait × mode scan. The systematic multi-trait discovery scan additionally applies level (iii), under which no new locus survives (Results/Discussion). Anchors were scored separately from new discoveries. Every reported hit additionally required an empirical $p$ from a covariate-aware Freedman–Lane permutation (re-whitening the shuffled phenotype each replicate, $(1+\sum)/(B+1)$ estimator with explicit REML-non-convergence handling) and an acceptable permutation-derived genomic-control $\lambda$. Covariates were panel-specific: the BnaGVD rapeseed flowering-time scan included ten genotype principal components in addition to the subgenome-stratified kinship null, and adding PCs did not change the cotton calibration (the kinship null already absorbs structure). A parametric bootstrap from the fitted kinship null ($B=20{,}000$) gave concordant tail behaviour. Per-panel $\lambda$ summaries are in `tables/lambda_perm_summary.tsv`; a degenerate mode with only two callable pairs ($G=2$, the sparse Horvath rapeseed panel's body/same scan) is tabulated but excluded from summaries.

**Robustness and influence diagnostics.** For each reported hit we confirmed the interaction was not a collinearity artifact: the variance-inflation factor of the product term relative to $[1,b_X,b_Y]$ was computed for the wheat triad (chr1 B–D 1.57 in the genome-wide context that matches the reported genome-wide $p$, chr5 A–D 2.27), and for the cotton and rapeseed discovery leads collinearity is precluded by construction — the cross-copy burden correlation of every reported pair lies far below the non-identifiable threshold (R2; median $|r|$ 0.04–0.11 across panels), so the product-term VIF is near one. In every case the signal is *pair-only*: no single-copy burden reaches genome-wide significance (cotton marginal $P>0.5$; rapeseed subgenome-A $P\approx0.86$, subgenome-C $P\approx0.04$, both far above the $2.9\times10^{-6}$ threshold, with the genome-wide marginal $p$-distribution uniform so the nominal C-copy value is null-consistent) while the product reaches $10^{-5}$–$10^{-7}$, confirming the interaction is not a main-effect alias, and a nested likelihood-ratio test confirmed the product improved fit beyond the two main effects. The rapeseed flowering-time lead additionally survived conditioning on the winter/spring/semi-winter ecotype and on local BnaA02 *FLC*-region additive tag SNPs (genome-wide significant across $P=3.3\to8.1\times10^{-7}$), ruling out the ecotype/*FLC* population structure that dominates rapeseed flowering time as the source of the signal. A development-panel cotton analysis additionally characterised per-observation influence by the maximum absolute DFBETA of the product coefficient under leave-one-sample refitting; for the CottonGVD and BnaGVD discovery leads the controlling robustness criterion is instead the covariate-aware permutation family-wise-error test reported in R4, together with the regional concordance of adjacent homoeolog pairs (rapeseed) and the cross-trait specificity of the null traits.

**Type-I behaviour under homoeolog collinearity and dominance.** Because homoeologous copies are correlated, the burden product $b_X\!\cdot\!b_Y$ becomes collinear with the squared per-copy burdens as cross-copy correlation $r$ rises, so an unmodelled per-copy dominance (curvature) main effect can alias into the interaction term. We quantified this with a calibration simulation on the production scan ($n=300$, $G=60$ gene pairs, 8 SNPs per copy, 1,000 replicates per cell), sweeping $r\in\{0.05,0.40,0.70,0.85,0.95\}$ across four arms — population structure, realistic burden-correlated additive main effects, an adversarial dominance main effect, and a power arm — each evaluated for the subgenome-stratified test and a no-GRM ordinary-least-squares comparator (Supplementary Table S2). Under the dominance arm the unadjusted product test leaked (genome-wide family-wise error rate rising from 0.07 toward 1.0 as $r\to1$); conditioning the per-pair design on the squared burdens, $y_w \sim 1 + b_X + b_Y + b_X^2 + b_Y^2 + b_X\!\cdot\!b_Y$ (an opt-in `dominance_adjust` flag, off by default so existing results are byte-identical), restored control near the nominal level across all arms in the identifiable regime. Calibration is claimed only where the design is identifiable (realized $r<0.90$ and a low fraction of degenerate fits); the singular limit $r\to1$ is reported as non-identifiable, not as calibration. On the real cotton panel the analytic ACAT genome-wide type-I error matched nominal at every level tested (0.0496/0.0102/0.0052 at $\alpha=0.05/0.01/0.005$; $\lambda_{GC}=0.999$; Supplementary Table S3).

**Power simulations.** Power was assessed on real cotton genotypes ($n=419$) by injecting sparse homoeolog-pair interactions at a fixed interaction PVE and measuring the rejection rate of the per-pair/ACAT test against alternatives, each at its own empirical size (Supplementary Tables S5–S8). Specificity was established by (i) a pairing-sanity sweep in which the homoeolog partner was progressively mis-paired (power decaying monotonically from 0.82 at the correct pairing to 0.11 at random pairing; Supplementary Table S4), and (ii) an architecture panel confirming the burden-product test responds to on-model block interactions but not to single-SNP-pair or sign-discordant architectures (Supplementary Table S5). Two y-independent extensions were characterised in simulation: a prior reweighting of pairs (weighted ACAT, below) raises detections at fixed family-wise error only when the prior is aligned with the truth (and not for a misaligned/random prior; Supplementary Table S6), and an across-trait ACAT raises pleiotropic detections only when interactions are genuinely shared across a frozen trait set (Supplementary Table S7).

**Benchmark and external tools.** A matched candidate-pair benchmark had six method families test the same synteny-guided pair universe ($G=80$, 200 replicates), each thresholded at its own held-out family-wise error rate, across scenarios (homoeolog burden-product; single causal SNP pair; additive-only anti-circularity; mixed-sign; mispaired-specificity). We additionally ran four published association tools — GWASpoly (Rosyara et al. 2016), regenie (Mbatchou et al. 2021), STAAR (Li et al. 2020) and SKAT (Wu et al. 2011) — plus a PLINK (Chang et al. 2015) SNP×SNP epistasis scan, on the CottonGVD cotton leads under prespecified thresholds. This is a *post-discovery, lead-locus* comparison: the loci were selected by *homoeogwas*, and each tool was handed a frozen candidate manifest (the in-gene SNPs of each lead pair, identical to the discovery inputs), so it tests whether each tool's native estimand recovers the same frozen signal — not an unbiased genome-wide discovery contest (that is the matched simulation benchmark). Each comparator carried its own structure correction: GWASpoly its polyploid kinship (`set.K`), regenie its step-1 LOCO polygenic null, and SKAT and STAAR ten genotype principal components added to the null model; the rare-variant aggregation tools (SKAT, STAAR, regenie burden) were applied to common variants as single-gene baselines (stated as such). Principal components were computed by `plink2 --pca` on LD-pruned markers, primarily from the genome-wide (A+D) set (9,207 pruned SNPs) with the A-subgenome-only set (6,392 SNPs) as a sensitivity check; the collapse of the uncorrected signals held under both. Detection was scored against two prespecified bars, a candidate-region Bonferroni and a genome-wide threshold. We separately record that the *uncorrected* (intercept-only) SKAT/STAAR nulls returned inflated $p$-values ($10^{-38}$–$10^{-43}$) that collapsed to non-significance ($P>0.2$ under either PC set) — behaviour consistent with population-structure confounding rather than a validated diagnosis of any single mechanism — and that the raw PLINK epistasis signal was likewise structure-sensitive. For PLINK we did not re-run a genome-wide PC-adjusted epistasis scan; instead we recomputed every candidate-region A×D SNP-pair interaction with the same principal components (a post-hoc covariate-adjusted check of the candidate grid, taking the minimum adjusted $p$) and evaluated it against the $\sim2.4\times10^{11}$-pair genome-wide SNP×SNP multiplicity (0.05 Bonferroni $\approx2\times10^{-13}$; the pair count is $8.05\times10^{5}$ A × $3.04\times10^{5}$ D markers). Per-gene complete-case sample sizes were 1,108–1,178 of 1,245 (tools used their native missingness handling). Full per-tool outputs, PC sources and thresholds are in `results/phase7/ext_baseline_cottongvd/` (`external_baselines_cottongvd.json`/`.tsv`).

**Marker-density and sequencing-depth design analysis.** We thinned whole-genome wheat markers, refit the callable-pair count $G$ (Poisson model of in-gene SNP coverage; held-out median relative error 3.5%; Supplementary Table S32), and combined per-pair power with coverage to obtain a coverage-adjusted discovery probability; ploidy enters as $\binom{s}{2}$ pairs per gene group. We additionally provide a parametric sequencing-depth pre-flight calculator (`homoeogwas design`) that maps a planned whole-genome-sequencing coverage to an expected callable-pair count and design band, separately for the marginal subgenome scan and the stricter homoeolog-interaction test (which requires confidently genotyping and distinguishing both copies and therefore needs greater depth), by chaining a Poisson per-genotype confident-call model to the validated density curve (Supplementary Table S36). This is a planning heuristic anchored to each panel's observed density, not an empirical depth calibration (no raw reads were used); it degrades to a sparse-genotyping boundary for the already-genotyped GBS/array panels (oat, the sparse Horvath rapeseed panel, strawberry), whose limit is marker density rather than depth.

**Deep-learning variant prior (optional; documented negative).** *homoeogwas* exposes an optional, phenotype-independent prior interface: any per-pair weight vector, frozen before association, can up-weight prioritised pairs in a weighted Bonferroni / weighted-ACAT scan (`interact.burden.weights`). We evaluated zero-shot priors from two pretrained genomic language models — PlantCaduceus (225M; single-nucleotide masked Caduceus) (Zhai et al. 2025) and AgroNT (1B; 6-mer) (Mendoza-Revilla et al. 2024) — scoring each variant by the masked log-likelihood ratio of the alternate versus reference allele ($|\mathrm{LLR}|$), with reference alleles harmonised to the genome (including reverse-complement strand matches) before scoring. Three pre-registered evaluations were uniformly negative. First, ranking suggestive single variants by an $|\mathrm{LLR}|$/association fusion showed no permutation-significant recall improvement on any of 12 panel–trait combinations under a nested leave-one-QTL-out, permutation-calibrated protocol (all permutation $p\ge0.43$; Supplementary Table S41); apparent historical lifts were $\beta$-selection artifacts. Second, because a homoeolog-pair prior requires comparable per-copy scores within the same triad, we re-scored all variants in 1:1:1 triad gene bodies ($\pm2$ kb) with PlantCaduceus (506,397 variants, 16,491/16,518 triads with $\ge2$ scored copies) and tested, by a triad-blocked permutation that shuffles per-copy burdens within each triad (48,068 within-triad copy-pairs), whether the lower-expressed homoeolog copy carries a higher $|\mathrm{LLR}|$ burden (the relaxed-selection hypothesis linking the readout to expression dominance). The association was null and well-powered (within-triad sign concordance 0.502 versus a permutation null of 0.500, empirical $p=0.25$; Spearman of within-triad $\Delta$burden versus $\Delta$expression-fraction $=-0.01$): this zero-shot $|\mathrm{LLR}|$ readout — which measures variant disruptiveness rather than regulatory dominance — does not explain measured homoeolog expression bias. (The two models' scores are moderately correlated, Spearman 0.61, and AgroNT entered the single-variant ensemble above; a second $|\mathrm{LLR}|$ model is therefore not expected to differ, so we did not re-run the dense scan with AgroNT.) We accordingly document the deep-learning prior but do not use it in the default model; the weighted-ACAT interface remains available for any aligned external prior, including measured homoeolog expression bias.

**Homoeolog expression bias (HEB).** Triad transcript ratios were computed from the expVIP wheat RNA-seq compendium (39 studies / 939 samples, kallisto TPM, IWGSC v1.1 IDs) (Borrill et al. 2016; Ramírez-González et al. 2018); HEB = subgenome transcript fraction and Euclidean imbalance from balance. For the chr1 candidate we used a pre-registered (addendum) heading-window definition (reproductive-stage spike tissues; study-balanced weighting; $\geq3$ expressed samples/study), tested the imbalance against a detectability-matched null (background triads matched on expression level, variability, missingness, gene length and callable-SNP count; coarsened strata + powered rank-distance KNN), and conditioned the genetic interaction on local additive structure within ±1 Mb (single SNPs, burdens and PCs). The expression atlas is a reference genotype, not the GWAS individuals; we therefore report triangulation, not mediation. **Candidate-gene annotation** used Ensembl Plants protein-feature domains (Pfam/PANTHER) and DIAMOND/BLASTp of each gene protein against the *Arabidopsis* TAIR10 proteome (closest comparative homolog by bitscore; low-confidence if identity $<30\%$ or query coverage $<50\%$); annotations are sequence-homology based, not functional validation. **Cotton fibre expression** of the two cotton lead pairs was read from two independent upland-cotton fibre developmental RNA-seq series — Zhang et al. (2015) and Hu et al. (2019) — via the COTTONOMICS atlas (HISAT2/StringTie normalised expression across ovule and 5/10/20/25-day-post-anthesis fibre; no 15-day sample exists in either series); the Gh_CRI v1 lead genes were mapped to the atlas TM-1 v2.1 gene models by reciprocal-best-hit (DIAMOND; all four leads matched 1:1 at 100% identity). For each pair we additionally computed a descriptive subgenome-balance profile across all tissue and fibre-developmental stages (per stage: total A+D expression, subgenome-A fraction A/(A+D), and log2 A/D ratio with a unit TPM pseudocount), reported separately for the two series; no genetic signal was used to select stages, so the profile is not circular. The atlas is from reference genotypes, not the GWAS individuals, so cotton expression is reported as a developmental correlate, not validation.

**Software, reproducibility and scalability.** *homoeogwas* is an open-source, pip-installable Python package (MIT licence) with a YAML-driven command-line interface and an equivalent agent/MCP server. The core workflow is `split` (assign a panel VCF to per-subgenome genotypes) → `fit` (per-subgenome GRMs, multi-kernel REML, optional LOCO, per-SNP scan) → `interact` (homoeolog-pair/triad burden-product scan, pairwise or multi-trait) → `calibrate` (permutation/bootstrap); auxiliary subcommands cover input preparation (`prep-snps`, `prep-homoeologs`), publication figures (`plot`, `locus`, `rplot`), and a parametric sequencing-depth pre-flight (`design`). On a diploid panel (one subgenome) the model degrades to a standard mixed-model GWAS. The package ships 358 passing unit tests (31 in the interaction engine; 3 further tests skipped where optional dependencies are absent), continuous-integration linting, and committed YAML run configurations plus public-data preparation scripts for the reported analyses. Large genotype panels and derived artifacts (per-subgenome PLINK, SNP-to-gene maps and homoeolog-pair tables) are not stored in the repository; the preparation scripts regenerate them from the cited public accessions and the committed configs then reproduce each scan, with expected result fingerprints provided for verification. The interaction scan is computationally light (≈35 s for 12,768 wheat triads = 38,304 pairwise tests; seconds for the few-thousand-pair cotton and rapeseed scans); at whole-genome scale the cost is dominated by genotype extraction and GRM construction (peak memory ≈6 GB array / ≈60 GB WGS; Supplementary Table S42).

**Statistics and reproducibility.** All interaction tests are two-sided; genome-wide significance is Bonferroni $0.05/G$ within each (trait × subgenome-pair) scan with Benjamini–Hochberg FDR reported across the trait grid. Per-figure sample sizes, the number of permutation/bootstrap replicates and the random seed are given in the corresponding figure legend and Supplementary Tables; permutations are covariate-aware Freedman–Lane (re-whitening each replicate) and the bootstrap is parametric from the fitted kinship null ($B=20{,}000$). No sample-size calculation, blinding or randomisation was applicable (observational association in existing germplasm panels). External tools were run at their default/prespecified thresholds with matched candidate-pair inputs; software and versions: plink2, bcftools, GEMMA, regenie, GWASpoly, STAAR, SKAT, mvMAPIT, PLINK (epistasis), DIAMOND and MCScanX (synteny), and PlantCaduceus/AgroNT via the pinned `transformers` build [versions in Supplementary Methods]. **Data availability.** The genotype/phenotype panels are public under their original accessions (wheat Watkins ENA PRJEB71453; cotton CottonGVD diversity panel, CNGB cottonGVD database [CITE/accession], and the HBAU NDM8 array panel; rapeseed BnaGVD whole-genome panel, rapeseed.biocloud.net [CITE/accession], and Horvath Zenodo 4302088; oat OLD; peanut PeanutPan; strawberry Fan 2022) and the wheat expVIP RNA-seq compendium (Borrill et al. 2016; Ramírez-González et al. 2018; constituent study accessions listed in Supplementary Table); reference assemblies and annotations are as cited. **Code availability.** *homoeogwas* is released under the MIT licence at https://github.com/Shipeng-Yang/HomoeoGWAS and distributed on PyPI (`pip install homoeogwas`).
